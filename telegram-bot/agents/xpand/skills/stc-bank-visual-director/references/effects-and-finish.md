# Effects and Finish — depth, blur, bokeh, atmosphere (with purpose only)

Core law: no effect is applied by default. Every effect must serve the message or the luxury, and must look like real optics captured by a real camera — never a filter slapped on top. When in doubt, remove it. A clean, correctly-lit realistic frame beats an effect-heavy one.

Write the effect you use, and its motivation, into the final prompt in optical terms (aperture, lens character, light source), not as "add blur."

---

## 1. Depth of field / bokeh (isolate the hero, signal premium)

Use when: you need to separate a hero (card, hand, phone/app, face) from a busy or supporting environment, or to add a premium, editorial feel.

How to do it right:
- Put the focal plane exactly on the message (the app screen, the card edge, the eyes). Everything the viewer must read stays sharp.
- Background blur falls off with distance — nearer background softer-but-legible, far background creamier. Not a single uniform smear.
- Real lens character: round or slightly cat-eye highlight bokeh, gentle vignette, natural highlight roll-off. State a plausible aperture/lens (e.g. "85mm at f/2, shallow depth of field, creamy natural bokeh").
- Foreground blur (shooting past an out-of-focus object) can add depth and premium framing — keep it subtle and on one side, often where copy will sit.

Avoid: flat Gaussian smear, blurred subject edges bleeding into background, a fake "portrait mode" halo around hair or a card, everything soft with nothing anchored.

## 2. Atmospheric depth (haze, volumetric light)

Use when: a Tier B/C studio or night scene needs air, separation, and luxury.

How to do it right:
- One soft, motivated light source creating a gentle volumetric beam or a light haze that separates the hero from a near-black background.
- Subtle — you feel depth, you don't see fog. Density fades with distance.
- Color the haze from the scene's real light (a faint purple glow in a Tier C set is fine as BACKGROUND light, never as a cast on the subject).

Avoid: theatrical smoke for its own sake, haze over the whole frame that lifts blacks everywhere, purple fog tinting skin or product.

## 3. Bloom / specular glow

Use when: there is a genuinely bright source (a window, a screen, a glossy metal edge, a rim light).

How to do it right:
- Bloom only around real bright points; a soft, tight falloff.
- Speculars on a card's metal band or glass are crisp and small.

Avoid: neon clipping, big soft halos, glow that has no light source, blown highlights with no detail.

## 4. Motion (only when motion IS the message)

Use when: the benefit is speed, arrival, delivery, or instant action.

How to do it right:
- Motion blur ONLY on the moving element (a passing car, a walking figure's trailing foot, a delivered package handed over). The hero the viewer must read stays sharp.
- Or freeze motion crisply (a splash, a tap on POS) with fast-shutter clarity if "instant" is the point.

Avoid: whole-frame blur, blurred hero, streaks that read as graphic speed-lines.

## 5. Reflections and gloss

- Match reflection sharpness to surface roughness and viewing angle (Fresnel: grazing angles reflect more).
- On glass and screens: coherent real reflections of the environment, dimmer than the source.
- On studio floors: a narrow, intentional, low-contrast reflection under the product.

Avoid: wet-floor mirror glare, plastic-that-looks-metallic, CGI over-gloss, double reflections that don't match the set.

## 6. Default realism finish (the base every ad ships with)

Even with zero "effects," ship this finish:
- Clean photographic micro-contrast and smooth tonal transitions.
- Subtle filmic highlight roll-off (no harsh clipping).
- Premium material separation (skin vs. fabric vs. metal vs. wood each read distinctly).
- Correct, coherent shadows and contact shadows; correct white balance on subject.
- Natural, restrained sharpening — no crunchy edges, no HDR halos, no visible noise.

## 7. Effect selection quick-guide by family (from stc-ad-dna.md)

- Family 1 (studio card): controlled semi-gloss + narrow reflection + optional shallow DOF on pedestal + subtle edge falloff. Optional faint volumetric beam.
- Family 2 (lifestyle): natural DOF on hands/faces; warm filmic roll-off; NO purple, NO haze, NO added glow.
- Family 3 (phone + real app): environment in gentle real bokeh so the app screen is sharp; coherent screen reflections; nothing else.
- Family 4 (surreal hybrid): real perspective + subtle haze for depth + motivated glow from within the metaphor only; never particles or light lines.

## 8. Final effect audit

- Does each effect make the message clearer or the frame more premium? If not, delete it.
- Does it look like real optics (real aperture, real light, real reflection)? If not, fix or delete.
- Is exactly one focal message sharp? Is the subject's color still true (no effect introduced a purple/green cast)?
