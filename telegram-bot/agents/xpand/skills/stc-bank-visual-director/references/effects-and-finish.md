# Lighting, shadows, reflections and finish
Specify the light's direction, apparent size and target. A large close source gives broad highlights and soft-edged cast shadows; small sources give tighter highlights and harder shadows. Contact shadows remain tight at contact and soften away from it. Fill lifts shadow detail; negative fill restores form without crushing blacks.
Purple set: illuminated top plane, darker vertical face, controlled backdrop wash and one coherent highlight source. Keep attractive specular reflections on metal/glass; use softer sheen on satin lacquer and broad diffuse gradients on matte paint. Natural violet bounce can appear on adjacent reflective edges without recoloring the whole product.
Real environments: motivated window/sky/practical key, believable falloff, neutral skin, location-specific warm/cool balance. Do not add purple lighting just to label the bank. Soft does not mean flat or uniformly blurred.
Reflection geometry: reflected object belongs to the same support plane and vanishing system; highlight position follows surface normal, source and camera. Roughness broadens/refines the reflection. Grazing dielectric surfaces often reflect more strongly; a polished dark floor need not look flooded with water. Avoid impossible mirror copies on matte fabric.
Clean finish: readable dark detail, smooth violet gradients, restrained local contrast, highlight roll-off, natural pores/fabric weave, precise product silhouette. No HDR halos, crunchy sharpening, color banding, synthetic skin or pasted cutout edges.
No decorative beams, drawn outlines, particles, sparkles, graphics or overlay blur. No atmospheric fog by default. Bokeh only when actual depth and aperture justify it; keep the hero and decisive interaction sharp. Real surface edges and specular strips are allowed physical evidence, not graphics.
