# STC BANK — TRAVEL CLAIM-PROOF IMMERSIVE STORYTELLING

This patch applies when the brief concerns travel, SIM, eSIM, roaming,
connectivity, mobility or access in every destination.

## The required shift

Do not create “a person travelling.” Create visual proof that:

`the traveller reached a meaningful place + is living the journey + the
service is naturally still available there`

Airport, train, hotel, passport, luggage, plane and foreign skyline are
category signals only. They say “travel”; they do not automatically say
“with you everywhere.”

## Destination proof

Ask: “Would this place make the viewer think: even here?” Prefer the strongest
credible answer over the most luxurious travel setting. Compare remote,
moving, foreign, difficult, unexpected and everyday-destination situations.

If replacing the environment with an office leaves the meaning unchanged, the
environment is decorative and must be redesigned.

## One story, not a prop collection

Use:

`one convincing destination + one natural user action + one subtle travel cue`

Do not stack passport, suitcase, plane, boarding pass, map, travel tag, phone
and skyline. Every object must support the same proof moment.

## Story over pose

Do not show a person sitting elegantly and displaying a phone. Define:

- what happened immediately before
- what is happening now
- what will happen next
- why this exact moment is visible

The subject should be task-focused, slightly imperfect and unaware of the
camera. Prefer a captured moment, not a model presentation.

## Immersive camera

Prioritize first-person POV, environmental POV, over-the-shoulder,
close-observational framing or a wide immersive shot when the promise is
experiential. The camera should make the viewer think “I am there,” not “I am
watching a model.”

## Hero hierarchy

Primary: the proof relationship — distinctive place + active service use +
continued journey.

Secondary: the phone or service.

Context: the destination and only the travel cues needed to understand it.

The phone is not the hero by default. For an every-destination claim, the
environment should occupy meaningful visual space and be noticed before the
device.

## Diegetic realism

Never add floating Wi-Fi icons, signal waves, location pins, arrows, network
symbols, glowing connection graphics, holograms or explanatory overlays in a
realistic scene. Connectivity must be proven through the situation and
natural service use.

The device must have correct scale, grip, perspective, screen plane,
foreshortening, occlusion, reflection and ambient brightness. It must not face
the camera like a billboard.

## First-second sequence

- 0–1 second: distinctive, remote or moving destination
- 1–2 seconds: natural service use inside that situation
- 2–3 seconds: the viewer understands that the service travelled with them

## Evaluation

Score claim proof /30, message clarity /20, storytelling /15, immersion /10,
natural product integration /10, memorability /10 and STC fit /5. Claim proof
below 24/30 fails regardless of the total.

Reject or redesign if:

- the place merely signals travel
- the subject presents the product
- a floating explanatory icon is used
- travel props are stacked
- there is no story event or continuity
- the phone dominates the frame
- the message depends on the headline
- the scene could be mistaken for tourism, travel booking, a lounge,
  credit-card payment or a generic banking app
