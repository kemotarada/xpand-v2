# STC BANK — CLAIM-PROOF SCENE ENGINE

This layer runs after message distillation and before Scene Architecture.
Its job is to turn a claim into visual evidence, not to select a location
that is merely associated with the category.

## Core chain

`claim → proof → scene → story → visual understanding`

Ask: “What situation would make the viewer believe this claim?” Do not ask
only: “What location is related to this service?”

## Claim distillation

For every brief define:

- claim: the exact promise
- functional proof: the real situation that demonstrates it
- human proof: what the customer is naturally doing
- environmental proof: the place that makes the promise harder to doubt
- emotional proof: what the viewer should feel

Use the structure:

`location + situation + action + benefit = claim proof`

## Proof location, not category location

A relevant location is not automatically a proof location. For reach,
availability, mobility, travel and connectivity, apply the “Even here?” test:
would the viewer think “even here?” and would the image answer “yes”?

Compare at least six proof situations internally when the location is not
specified. Include non-obvious alternatives before accepting the obvious
airport/city/studio answer. Rank locations by:

`claim proof 30 + human story 20 + message clarity 20 + memorability 10 +
emotion 10 + STC fit 5 + freshness 5`

A spectacular place with low proof strength loses to a less obvious place that
tests the promise.

## Story before pose

Reject or repair scenes that can be described as “a person standing and
holding the product.” Define what happened ten seconds before, what is
happening now and what will happen ten seconds later. The person must feel
like they are living the benefit, not presenting it to a photoshoot.

Prefer POV, over-the-shoulder, candid observation or close environmental
interaction when the claim is experiential. The camera should make the viewer
feel present inside the proof.

## Proof density and prop discipline

Use a small number of natural, story-driven props. Every major element must
support the same claim. Do not assemble a pile of category symbols:
airport + plane + passport + suitcase + phone is travel decoration unless a
single event connects them.

For “your SIM is with you in every destination”, a remote destination,
journey evidence, natural service use and environmental difficulty can all
support one meaning: “even here, it works.” A random landmark, generic
suitcase, decorative airplane or phone held toward camera does not.

## Product integration

The device must appear as part of the action:

- natural hand angle
- POV or over-the-shoulder use
- subtle, non-readable screen visibility
- correct scale, perspective, reflections and light

Never use an oversized phone, billboard-like screen, artificial product
presentation or readable UI as the proof.

## Message drift and copy independence

After building the scene ask:

1. What service would a viewer think this advertises?
2. Is that the intended service?
3. Would the claim still be suggested if the headline disappeared?
4. Would removing the location weaken the claim?

If a travel/SIM scene reads as payment, it fails. If a merchant scene reads as
a catalogue of terminal, phone and boxes, it fails. One dominant
interpretation is required.

## Camera alignment

- POV: personal experience is the proof
- wide environmental: distance, scale or remoteness is the proof
- close observational: natural service use is the proof
- over-the-shoulder: product and environment both need to read

The final order is:

`brief → message distillation → claim proof → six proof locations →
location ranking → story moment → camera → mutation → brand translation →
final prompt → visual QA`
