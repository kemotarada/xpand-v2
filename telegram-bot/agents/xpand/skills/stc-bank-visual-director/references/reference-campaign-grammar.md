# STC Bank Reference-Derived Campaign Grammar

This file converts the supplied STC Bank campaign examples into reusable art-direction rules. It is not a catalogue of compositions to copy.

## What the examples consistently do

- Start from one concrete proposition: travel, cashback, card choice, digital products, finance, restaurant value, trip splitting, support or transfer.
- Build one immediate hero relationship. The product is causal evidence, not decorative inventory.
- Use a clear three-level read: hero action or object, contextual proof, calm area for later headline and terms.
- Switch visual language according to the proposition instead of forcing every service into a purple room.
- Keep the Saudi/Gulf context credible through casting, wardrobe, interiors, behavior and light—not clichés.
- Use STC purple and green as controlled brand anchors. In realistic scenes, purple is often motivated practical light, a surface, a garment accent or a tonal bridge rather than a full-frame wash.

## Campaign structures observed

### 1. Benefit-in-use realism
A person is shown during the exact moment the benefit matters: preparing to travel, managing a trip, receiving finance, transferring money or getting support. Use natural gesture, useful props, believable wardrobe and location-specific depth. The phone may appear only when its action is relevant.

### 2. Decisive product action
Use macro or medium-close framing around one action: taking a card from a pocket, holding a useful app in context, carrying two offer items. Hands must be anatomically credible and interact with weight, fabric and objects correctly.

### 3. Branded purple studio narrative
Use connected violet planes and category objects with tactile realism. Cloth folds, eyewear, packaging or product materials can carry the occasion. Supports must share perspective and cast attached shadows. Never default to a blank podium with a device.

### 4. Product-as-threshold
A card, doorway or other product-related object may become a physically coherent transition into travel or access. The transformation must use one perspective system, one light logic and convincing contact. It is a single mechanism, not fantasy effects.

### 5. Premium contextual still life
Allowed only when the selected objects identify the category before copy. Use one hero cluster, deliberate asymmetry, tactile materials, edge highlights and 25–40% integrated copy-safe space. Reject a random luxury-object collection.

## Two-layer communication

**Image layer:** communicate the service category, audience moment and emotional payoff in under two seconds.

**Typography layer:** later communicates the exact amount, percentage, code, CTA, terms and logos. Therefore image-generation prompts contain no writing, logos, numbers or readable UI, while the composition intentionally reserves a calm region for later layout.

## Shot selection by message

- Human convenience or lifestyle: over-the-shoulder, eye-level three-quarter, first-person POV or observational medium shot.
- Product ownership or premium card: macro action, tight crop, low oblique product shot or hand interaction.
- Travel access: threshold/reveal, POV from a credible travel environment, or environmental portrait.
- Studio offer/category: elevated three-quarter, low oblique or controlled frontal perspective with connected planes.
- Complex service ecosystem: one continuous causal scene, never split-screen, collage, HUD or diagram.

## Red flags

Reject device museums, centered app screenshots without meaningful context, random landmark collages, generic smiling office users, purple fog, fake UI, floating cards, decorative particles, neon network lines, unrelated props, excessive symmetry, dead-wall copy space and any concept that needs a caption to explain why its objects are together.

## Release test

Release only when all are true:
- one dominant hero;
- one benefit category readable without copy;
- one causal banking cue;
- one selected campaign structure;
- one camera that reinforces the message;
- physically credible hands, materials, light, occlusion and contact;
- integrated copy-safe space;
- no generated text, logo, readable UI or decorative overlay.
