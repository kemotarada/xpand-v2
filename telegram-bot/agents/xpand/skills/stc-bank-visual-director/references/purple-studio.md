# Purple studio: geometry, color and premium finish
A stage is a connected spatial system, not a flat purple backdrop pasted behind an object. Select ONE primary reference for hue and lighting. The uploaded card series has dark violet borders, saturated violet illuminated planes, near-black product bodies and neutral metallic specular bands. The fashion reference is brighter with warm coral fabric; do not blend these into a muddy average.
Build two or three planes with distinct values: lit top, midtone side, dark recess. Use thin bevels and consistent edge thickness, not luminous drawn outlines. A support may rise through tiers when it clarifies product hierarchy. Leave the upper quadrant quiet, preserving a gradual light field.
Perspective: choose the camera and horizon first. World-parallel platform and product edges converge to the SAME vanishing point; vertical objects have vertical axes, horizontal cards lying on a plane inherit its homography. A diagonal in the picture may come from object yaw, a ramp, or camera roll: specify which. Do not label every diagonal a Dutch angle. A card leaning on a base requires an actual contact edge/back support and an attached shadow. Plane changes cause believable occlusion.
Purple planes may occupy most of the studio frame. Obtain hue from the selected image, not the brand name. JPEG swatches are observational, not official brand specifications. Preserve saturation in midtones while rolling highlights toward lighter violet. Do not crush all shadows to black or shift into blue/cyan/magenta indiscriminately.
Lighting recipe: large soft key above and to one side; weak fill revealing the dark product; a controlled strip reflection across metal/glass; separate broad wash on the rear plane. Maintain separation with local contrast rather than stronger neon. Reflections extend from the actual contact plane with correct foreshortening; semi-gloss softens reflected detail, satin spreads a broad sheen, matte faces keep diffuse gradients. Do not give fabric the reflectivity of acrylic.
Viewpoint families: elevated three-quarter for a card on a sloping plane; modest low-angle for upright hero/tiers; near-level oblique for a reflective shelf; three-quarter still life for draped fabric. Overhead and worm's-eye are optional extensions, not proven camera setups for all references. Keep card thickness thin, corner radii consistent and product surfaces unlettered.
